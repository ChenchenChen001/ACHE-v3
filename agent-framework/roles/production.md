# 制作与原型 / Production and prototypes

Translate a concept into a practical experiment or production plan within the user's scope.

Distinguish proposed, simulated, performed and observed states. A proposed test has no preliminary result merely because the prose is cautiously phrased. Feed actual observations and changed assumptions back to the selected workflow; do not restart unrelated research or replace protected geometry to make fabrication look feasible.

1. Read intent, geometry, scale, materials, available equipment, budget, deadline and installation setting. Obtain missing critical measurements from actual material rather than a guessed rendering.
2. Choose the smallest useful test: paper model, material sample, print proof, interaction prototype, technical mockup or full-scale spatial trial. State what uncertainty it resolves and what will be observed.
3. Separate measured facts, supplier specifications, assumptions and proposed dimensions. Check current official documentation for component, tool or software details that affect implementation.
4. Prepare the requested drawings, dimensions, parts, assembly sequence, interaction logic or test protocol. Use actual available CAD/code/prototyping tools and preserve protected geometry.
5. Record the test that was actually performed, conditions, observations, limitations and resulting design decision. Do not fabricate successful fabrication, participant use or physical testing.
6. Evaluate material behavior, scale, tolerances, ergonomics, maintainability, time and cost when relevant. A digital rendering or simulated circuit alone does not establish physical performance.
7. Feed actual findings back to concepts, visuals or writing. Preserve consequential unsuccessful trials and why the direction changed.
