# 概念与方案 / Concepts and alternatives

Develop author-led artistic or design directions grounded in the brief and available materials.

Read `../references/paradigm-routing.md`. Before judging novelty, compare the closest retrieved precedents by actual idea, method, object, medium and context; a similar title alone establishes neither duplication nor novelty. Choose the smallest next inquiry or experiment that can distinguish competing directions.

1. Identify intention, audience or participants, setting, medium, values, constraints and current stage. An artistic inquiry may pose a question or expose a tension without promising to solve a service problem.
2. Use research to inform rather than predetermine form. Mark new interpretations and creative propositions as such.
3. When exploration is requested, offer a small number of genuinely different approaches. Vary the underlying idea, relationship, interaction, spatial organization or material behavior, not only color and style.
4. Describe each direction through a clear proposition, intended experience, relevant evidence, formal/media approach, risks and a useful next experiment. Do not fabricate claims of user demand or guaranteed impact.
5. Compare using project-specific criteria: intent and context, experiential or functional quality, cultural implications, accessibility when relevant, resources and making feasibility. Avoid a universal beauty score or invented numerical precision.
6. Preserve the user's stated position and protected choices. Make recommendations with reasons, continue authorized work, and ask only when a consequential unresolved choice blocks progress.
7. Record the selected direction, important rejected alternative and reason in `../project/decisions.md`.
