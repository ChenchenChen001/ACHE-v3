# 问题驱动调研 / Question-led research

## Define the brief

For a substantive investigation, establish research questions, scope, intended reader and the decision the research informs. Derive an adequate working brief from available context and continue; ask only if a material ambiguity blocks the task. A quick reference lookup does not require a literature-review protocol.

## Search perspectives

Select perspectives relevant to the question: the dominant account, counterarguments, historical/cultural context, methods, situated practice, and affected communities. Use different keywords and follow references from relevant sources. No fixed count of papers or perspectives establishes coverage. When parallel agents are authorized, give separate search questions; otherwise investigate the perspectives serially.

Distinguish normal zero-hit responses from failed tools, paywalls, timeouts and inaccessible archives. An empty search is not proof that a topic is new. Compare the closest works on contribution, object, method, context and material, not title similarity alone.

## Extract and compare

For each useful work, record the relevant finding or proposition, method/medium, conditions, source location, and relationship to other works. Mark what was actually read. Use `project/sources.json` for material sources and `claims.json` for argument-bearing factual statements; use an evidence link for the specific support or qualification, not merely a topical source ID.

Organize by research question, argument, theme or method. Compare works within the argument rather than producing a sequence of isolated summaries. Explain contradictory findings through their contexts and methods where possible. If the evidence does not decide, retain the disagreement.

For art/design, permit overlapping themes, annotated portfolios, image sequences and material taxonomies. Do not force mutually exclusive categories or discard a sparse but conceptually important dimension to make a table look complete.

## Check and repair

- Scope: does the output answer the actual research questions?
- Coverage: are relevant schools, contexts, sources or counterexamples missing?
- Citation: are entries real, metadata accurate, and the cited claim supported?
- Synthesis: does the organization produce a reasoned comparison or interpretation?
- Calibration: do certainty and generality match the material?
- Connection: can a reader see how the evidence relates to the user's next decision?

Repair the failed part: refine the question, add a targeted search, correct a citation, regroup material or revise a claim. Do not restart every search or endlessly add references after sufficient coverage is reached.

## Deliver

A substantive report includes the brief, a concise search scope, thematic comparisons, disagreements, implications for the project, unresolved gaps and usable sources. A short request gets a short answer. Do not call a selective source scan a systematic review. Keep research uncertainty in the report and project records; do not scatter mechanical warnings through eventual authored prose.
